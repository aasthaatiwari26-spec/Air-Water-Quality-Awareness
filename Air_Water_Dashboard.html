Why visualization helps

About this project

This project studies how residents perceive air and water quality in their own neighborhoods — not sensor readings, but lived experience: what people notice, what they think is causing it, and how it affects their health.

Why we did this

Pollution data is often technical and hard to relate to. We wanted to capture what people actually notice in daily life — bad smells, visible dust, discoloured water — and see if those perceptions line up across neighborhoods.

How the data was collected

A Google Form was shared with residents across several areas, asking about air quality ratings, how often pollution is noticed, likely sources, water quality, and any health issues experienced.

What the dashboard shows

  • Self-reported air quality ratings by area
  • How often people notice pollution
  • What residents believe is the major source
  • Health problems people associate with it
  • Water quality perception and concerns

Tools used

  • Google Forms for data collection
  • Google Sheets for storing responses
  • This dashboard for visualization

Methodology & limitations

This is a self-reported perception survey, not a sensor-based measurement — ratings reflect how people feel about air and water quality, not verified AQI or water-testing data. The sample skews toward the 18–25 age group and a handful of Mumbai-region neighborhoods, so it isn't representative of the wider city. We think it's still useful: it captures lived experience that official data often misses, and it's a starting point for comparing perception against measured pollution data in future work.

            Awareness Tips

            Check before you step outLook up your area's AQI on days you'll be outdoors for long — reschedule intense outdoor activity if it's high.
            Ventilate smartlyOpen windows when outdoor air is cleaner (early morning/late night); keep them shut during peak traffic hours.
            Filter your waterIf your source shows visible impurities or odour, use a proper filter and get it tested periodically.
            Reduce burning wasteAvoid burning garbage or leaves — it's a major, avoidable contributor to local air pollution.
            Report visible issuesDiscoloured water or heavy dust/smoke near you is worth reporting to local municipal authorities.
            Track symptomsPersistent coughing, eye irritation or headaches tied to certain days/areas are worth noting and discussing with a doctor.
